Engineer, analyst, PM, MBA: why I went to Cambridge

On paper my path looks like a series of left turns. I studied Materials Science and Engineering at IIT Kanpur, joined a fintech as a risk associate, spent two years as an analyst at an EdTech company, moved into product at an AI entertainment startup, and now I'm doing an MBA at Cambridge Judge Business School. People ask how those connect. The honest answer is that each step followed the same question, asked more broadly every time: why do people behave the way they do with a product, and what can you do about it?

Engineering taught me to think in systems

Materials science is about how structure at one scale produces behaviour at another. You learn to break a messy problem into mechanisms, form a hypothesis, test it, and be suspicious of your own results. I didn't stay in the field, but I kept the habit. Most of the analytics and product work I've done since has been the same loop with different raw material.

Analytics taught me where the truth lives

At Uni Cards I was analysing transaction data to reduce false positives in a fraud model. At Masai I built data pipelines, funnel frameworks, and a model to predict student outcomes. That was where I learned that data work only matters when it changes a decision. A beautiful dashboard nobody acts on is decoration. A rough analysis that changes how an admissions team spends its week is worth far more.

It also taught me to get close to the people behind the numbers. Every metric is someone's behaviour, summarised. The best analysis I did came from sitting with the teams who owned the outcome, not from writing better SQL.

Product taught me to own the outcome

At Dashverse I started as a Product Analyst, building the analytics foundation, and moved into an Associate Product Manager role working on retention, lifecycle, and monetisation. The difference was ownership. As an analyst you recommend. As a PM you decide, and you live with the result. That shift taught me more about prioritisation, trade-offs, and working across teams than anything before it.

Why an MBA, and why now

Four years in, I could see the edges of what I knew. I'm comfortable with data and product, but the decisions I want to make next involve strategy, finance, and leading people. Those are harder to learn on the job when the job keeps you deep in execution. I wanted a year to step back, learn the parts I'd been picking up informally, and test my instincts against people from completely different industries.

Cambridge appealed for a few reasons: a small, international class, a strong connection to the technology and venture ecosystem around the city, and live consulting projects with real organisations. I learn best by doing, and the programme is built around that.

What I'm hoping to get out of it

  • Range. A grounding in finance, strategy, and organisations to sit alongside the product and data skills I already have.
  • Perspective. Working with people who've built careers in sectors and countries I haven't.
  • Direction. A clearer view of the problems I want to spend the next decade on, most likely at the intersection of product, data, and AI.

I'll be writing about what I learn along the way, both from the MBA and from the work that got me here. If any of this overlaps with what you're working on, I'd be glad to hear from you.
