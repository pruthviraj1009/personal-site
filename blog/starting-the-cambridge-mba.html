Starting the Cambridge MBA: what I want to get out of it

I've just started the MBA at Cambridge Judge Business School. This is the first in an occasional series of notes from the year. Partly to share what it's like, mostly to keep myself honest about what I came here to do.

Why write this down now

An MBA year is short and very full. There are more talks, clubs, projects, and people than anyone has time for, and it's easy to drift toward whatever is loudest that week. Writing my goals down at the start gives me something to check against when I'm deciding how to spend my time later.

What I want to learn

  • Finance and strategy, properly. I've worked with growth and product metrics for years but have mostly picked up the finance and strategy side informally. I want the foundations.
  • Leading people. As a PM I led through influence. I want to understand how organisations actually work, and how to lead teams well as my roles grow.
  • How others make decisions. My experience is in startups in India. Classmates come from consulting, healthcare, energy, government, and dozens of countries. I want to learn how they approach problems.

What I want to build

I learn best by doing. The programme's live consulting projects with real organisations are one of the main reasons I chose it. Alongside those, I want to keep building things myself, especially small AI-assisted tools and prototypes, and see how far that can go when paired with what I'm learning in class.

What I'm unsure about

The honest answer is: a lot. The clearest open question is what I want to do after the year ends. I'm drawn to product leadership in technology, particularly where data and AI change what a product can do, but I'm deliberately keeping that open. I'd rather test a few directions than commit early.

How I'll know if it was worth it

Not by a job title. I'll count the year a success if, at the end of it, I can make better decisions in areas I currently find uncomfortable, I've built relationships with people I'd want to work with for the rest of my career, and I have a clearer answer to what I want to spend the next decade on.

I'll check back in at the end of term.
