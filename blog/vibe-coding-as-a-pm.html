Vibe coding as a PM: building this site with AI

This site was built almost entirely through conversation with an AI coding assistant. I described what I wanted, reviewed what it produced, pushed back when something wasn't right, and repeated. I'm not a software engineer. I've written SQL and Python for analysis, but I'd never built and deployed a website from scratch. A few years ago this would have been a several-week project. It took a handful of evenings.

"Vibe coding" is the half-joking name for this way of working. Here's what it was like and what I think it means for people in product roles.

What I built

The site is deliberately simple: plain HTML, CSS, and a few lines of JavaScript, no framework and no build step. It runs on my own server behind Caddy, which handles HTTPS automatically. Along the way I added the things a real site needs: an RSS feed, a sitemap, social-share images generated by a small Python script, structured metadata for search engines, accessibility fixes, and caching rules so visitors see updates straight away.

I knew most of those things existed. I didn't know how to build any of them. The assistant did, and it explained what it was doing when I asked.

What it's good at

  • Getting from nothing to something. The blank page disappears. You can see and react to a working version in minutes.
  • Filling in the boring but essential parts. Meta tags, feed formats, server config. It knows the conventions so you don't have to look them up.
  • Explaining as it goes. When I asked why something was done a certain way, I got an answer. I learned more about how the web works this way than I would have from a tutorial.
  • Review. Asking it to check its own work for bugs and accessibility problems caught real issues.

Where you still need judgement

It's fast, but it isn't a substitute for knowing what you want. The assistant will happily build whatever you describe, including the wrong thing. The quality of the result tracked the clarity of my instructions almost exactly. Vague requests got generic output. Specific requests, with examples and constraints, got what I needed.

You also need to check what it produces. It can be confidently wrong, and it can produce placeholder content that looks finished. Everything that goes out under your name needs a human read.

What this means for PMs

If the quality of the output depends on the clarity of the spec, then vibe coding is mostly a product management skill. Defining the problem, breaking it into steps, specifying what good looks like, and reviewing the result is exactly what PMs do every day.

The difference is the feedback loop. Instead of writing a spec and waiting for a sprint, you can see your idea working in minutes and refine it. For prototyping, internal tools, and testing whether an idea is worth a team's time, that changes what a single PM can do.

It doesn't replace engineers for anything that needs to scale, be secure, or be maintained by a team. But it closes much of the gap between having an idea and seeing it work. I plan to explore it further during my MBA, and I expect to write more about it.
